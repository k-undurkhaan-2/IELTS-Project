# Content Policy

This policy separates software licensing from rights in educational, media, reference, and brand material used with IELTMPS.

It is a public framework, not a declaration that every existing repository asset has completed a rights review.

## Software and content are different

An open-source software license governs the code and other files within its stated scope. It does not automatically grant permission to copy, publish, adapt, perform, stream, or sell unrelated content that the software loads or displays.

Separately controlled material may include:

- Examination-style questions and answer sets.
- Articles and other source texts.
- Audio and transcripts.
- PDFs and images.
- Explanations and generated explanations.
- Fonts, dictionaries, and wordlists.
- Logos, names, and trade marks.
- Data imported or supplied by a user or deployment operator.
- Private runtime resources delivered outside the public source repository.

Each item needs an independent legal basis for the intended use.

## Content permitted for publication

A public IELTMPS service or release should publish content only when it is:

- Original content created by a person who has authorized the intended publication.
- Used under a license that permits the intended distribution and service use.
- In the public domain in the relevant jurisdiction and used consistently with applicable law.
- Used under another documented permission or legal basis appropriate to the intended audience and territory.

A source being accessible online does not establish permission. Removing a credit, changing formatting, generating a bundle, or creating an explanation also does not remove underlying rights.

Content-source records should identify the source, rightsholder where known, permission or license, applicable notice, review date, permitted uses, and any geographic or service restrictions.

## Open-source rights

Broad noncommercial restrictions must not be added to software covered by GNU GPL or AGPL terms. Service policies cannot reduce the software freedoms granted by an applicable open-source license.

Service operators may separately limit access to or reuse of content when those limits reflect actual content rights, privacy obligations, abuse controls, or service terms. Those content limits should be identified as content or service conditions rather than software-license restrictions.

Fees for hosting, infrastructure, support, third-party providers, or resource-intensive services do not create exclusive rights to applicable open-source source code.

## Responsibility of service operators

The maintainer-operated service is responsible for the content it selects and publishes. Before public operation, it should establish:

- A content intake and rights-review process.
- Source and permission records.
- Clear attribution where required.
- Review for sensitive, personal, harmful, or misleading material.
- A correction and takedown contact.
- A process for responding to credible rights complaints.
- A record of removals, replacements, and notice updates.
- Periodic review of content whose permission or source may have changed.

The project’s public-interest orientation does not create an exemption from copyright, trade-mark, privacy, or other applicable law.

## Responsibility of third-party users and self-hosters

Third-party users and self-hosters are responsible for content they add, import, publish, or make available. Availability of the IELTMPS source code does not authorize their examination material, media, branding, or learner data.

An operator should confirm that its intended use—including public access, copying, adaptation, audio streaming, or charging for service resources—is permitted. It should also provide its own attribution, privacy information, support, and takedown channel.

The IELTMPS maintainers do not endorse a third-party deployment merely because it uses the software.

## Public releases and private runtime resources

Public releases must exclude:

- Private runtime resources without verified distribution permission.
- Deployment secrets and operator-specific configuration.
- User records, database exports, backups, and logs.
- Hidden-service keys and client-auth material.
- Content whose publication basis has not been documented.

Release tooling and manifests should fail closed when a private resource is not explicitly approved. Generated files must be traced back to their source material and must preserve applicable notices.

A generated exam, explanation, transcript, transformed image, or compiled data bundle is not automatically free of the rights attached to its inputs.

## Current inventory limitations

The repository has not completed a full rights inventory for all existing:

- Fonts.
- Images.
- Wordlists and vocabulary data.
- Dictionaries.
- Generated exams.
- Source articles.
- Explanations and agent-assisted explanations.
- Listening questions, audio, and related media.
- Historical or compatibility assets.

Some third-party notices are present, including notices associated with selected vendor and dictionary material. Their presence does not classify every neighboring file.

Until the inventory is complete, public-service and release reviews must treat uncertain content conservatively. This policy does not claim that all currently bundled material is cleared for public operation.

## Questions, explanations, and assessment claims

IELTMPS is an independent practice project. Content should not imply endorsement by an examination owner or represent a practice result as a certified examination score.

Explanations should distinguish source facts, editorial interpretation, and machine-assisted output where relevant. A generated explanation should be reviewed for accuracy, rights, and learner safety before public publication.

## Trade marks and identity

IELTS-related names, logos, and marks belong to their respective owners. IELTMPS must remain clearly independent and must not use official logos or presentation that suggests affiliation, sponsorship, endorsement, or examination authority.

The IELTMPS name does not grant rights to third-party marks appearing in historical code, content, or documentation.

## Correction and takedown direction

Before a public Hosted Service launch, the project should publish a contact and procedure for:

1. Identifying the material and its location.
2. Describing the claimed right or harm.
3. Preserving enough information to investigate.
4. Restricting or removing material when appropriate.
5. Correcting notices or source records.
6. Recording the decision and any later restoration.

Detailed legal and operational procedures require review for the jurisdictions and service model involved.
